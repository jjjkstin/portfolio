Princeton, NJ

Hi, I’m JaeHa (Justin) Kim, a member of Princeton University’s Class of 2030 and an intended A.B. candidate in Public and International Affairs, with prospective minors in Statistics & Machine Learning and Finance.

I’m interested in how data and policy intersect, especially around immigrant communities, identity, and mental health. As a research intern at the CHAMP Lab at Teachers College, Columbia University, and the SLAAY Lab at Montclair State University, I studied the experiences of Asian American and Latino youth and families, using R for quantitative analysis. That work led to a coauthored peer-reviewed paper on racial discrimination, resilience, and mental health among Asian American youth, and to my own case study of a DACA recipient’s journey in the United States.

Looking ahead, I’m especially interested in AI governance and AI policymaking. I’m currently writing an independent paper on AI-generated animation and what it means for art, including the emotional value of AI-generated artworks in the future.

Outside the classroom, I founded and led Koread, a Korean-American news platform with 18 team members across three continents. At Princeton, I’m on the Whitman College Council, copy edit for the Nassau Weekly, and take part in the James Madison Program UFF, Christian Union Nova, and WaWa United Soccer.

I speak English and Korean (bilingual) and Spanish (advanced).

selected publications

  1. NHSJS
    Building Resilience and Identity in a Land of Uncertainty: A Case Study of a DACA Recipient’s Journey in the United States
    JaeHa Kim
    National High School Journal of Science, 2025
    Abs HTML PDF

    A qualitative case study, based on a semi-structured interview with a 26-year-old DACA recipient from Costa Rica. Using thematic analysis and community cultural wealth theory, it identifies four themes: immigration journey and status awareness, educational barriers and resilience, identity formation and cultural integration, and policy perspectives and future efforts.

  2. JSR
    Unraveling the Relationship Between Racial Discrimination, Resilience, and Mental Health: A Study of Asian American Youth
    Joowan Byun, Josephine Yoo, Danny Paik, and 2 more authors
    Journal of Student Research (High School Edition), 2024
    Abs DOI HTML

    Examines how racial discrimination during the COVID-19 pandemic affected anxiety and depression among 72 Asian American youth, and whether resilience moderated that link.
